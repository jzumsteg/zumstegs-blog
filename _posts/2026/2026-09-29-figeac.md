We spent a couple nights in Figeac twelve years ago (we think) and liked it a lot. This year, we were in the area&mdash;kind of&mdash; and decided to see Figeac again. We ended up spending five nights here, which is longer than we usually spend in smaller towns, but we didn't regret that at all. Figeac is an enjoyable place and the area around it is beautiful; perfect for drives a couple of days.

Figeac fits our description of a perfect French town to explore. It's been here forever: archeologists have found a Roman road down the Célé river valley where Figeac is situated. It has not been restored and prettified for tourists (though we did see a few more tourist-oriented stores than on our last trip). It feels more as if Figeac has been well-maintained over the centuries rather than restored and re-vamped to something that would draw tourists.

Can you see why we like Figeac so much? It's just an authentic, living town; nothing fancy or pretentious. We spent five nights here - a long stay for us - and didn't regret it at all. It didn't hurt that we were in a small "boutique" hotel that was very nice and was run by a super-nice couple. 

I should mention the Musée Champollion. Champollion was the person who decoded Egyptian hieroglyphics in 1822. He was born in Figeac and his house is now a museum. There's a large room dedicated to him and how he decoded the hieroglyphics. The rest of the museum is dedicated to the history of writing, from marks on clay tablets to modern alphabets and languages. It was fascinating! Worth a trip to Figeac by itself.

We'll probably not spend five nights in Figeac again, but if we're in the area? Two or three nights, for sure. We love the place.
<h2>Rambles Around Figeac</h2>
One thing we remembered from our earlier stay here was drives we took in the area. Figeac sits in the Célé River valley, and is north of the Lot River valley. So one day we drove down the Célé Valley and then back to Figeac through the Lot Valley.
